# Visual Language Rules

## Purpose
Create a consistent original sci-fi rhythm-game identity.

## Shape Language
Use a controlled vocabulary of large architectural silhouettes, modular structures, clean geometric forms, consistent edge treatments, and selected accent shapes.

- Design major silhouettes before small detail.
- Use primitive meshes only when appropriate to the final visual language or for blockout.
- Avoid arbitrary floating cubes, disconnected cylinders, random platforms, and unrelated geometric decoration.
- Reuse a coherent family of proportions, bevels, panel divisions, joints, and material transitions.
- Avoid excessive greebles that do not read at the intended viewing distance.

## Color
Document:
- Environment base colors.
- Gameplay colors and their meanings.
- Hazard/obstacle colors.
- Accent and emissive colors.
- Background brightness and contrast limits.
- Allowed color transitions.

Never change a gameplay color's meaning merely for variety. Keep gameplay cues distinguishable under all intended lighting states.

## Materials and Emission
- Use emissive materials for meaningful luminous features, not as a universal decoration.
- Control bloom and exposure to preserve silhouettes and edge definition.
- Keep material roughness, metallic response, and transparency consistent with the art direction.
- Limit overlapping transparent effects.
- Prefer large readable forms over noisy surface detail.

## Composition
Design foreground/gameplay, environment/midground, and background/atmosphere as distinct layers. Use negative space intentionally. Establish a clear focal hierarchy and avoid competing focal points.

## Acceptance Criteria
The scene reads as one art-directed environment from the intended camera. Gameplay cues remain distinct and background detail does not overwhelm the primary forms.
